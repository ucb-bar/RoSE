On-SoC flight-control architecture

The whole flight stack — sensing, EKF state estimation, the fused vision policy, and TinyMPC control — runs on the simulated RISC-V SoC (spike). It steps in lockstep with the IsaacLab warehouse gate scene, consuming sensors and returning four motor thrusts. This is the loop that flies the course.

The pipeline, stage by stage

Sensing

read_nav_frame

Pulls accel, gyro, optical-flow and downward-ToF from IsaacLab over the bridge each tick — the same reads the real riskybird firmware makes from its IMU / flow / ToF drivers.

200 Hz

State estimation

EKF

Mahony attitude filter (gyro + accel) plus a per-axis Kalman filter fusing flow-velocity and ToF-altitude into a 12-state estimate. Altitude initialized from the first ToF; heading seeded once at takeoff.

200 Hz

Vision policy

fused model

int8 per-channel convolutional encoders + an fp16 LSTM tail on curated RVV kernels, over the FPV frame, cross-ToF and low-dim state → a (yaw-rate, forward-speed) command, held between runs.

20 Hz · ZOH

Control

TinyMPC

Derotates the EKF velocity into the body-heading frame, applies the vision command as a body-frame velocity + yaw-rate setpoint with an alignment gate and altitude hold, and solves for four clamped rotor thrusts.

200 Hz

Actuation

send_control

Emits the four thrusts back over the bridge. IsaacLab converts each to a per-rotor force and sums the propeller-drag yaw torque onto the airframe, then steps physics once.

200 Hz

Four fixes that made it fly the course

01 Yaw torque to the airframe

Propeller-drag yaw torque was being applied to the free-spinning prop bodies, where it vanished. Summing it onto the base frame gave the controller real yaw authority.

02 Fly at the trained altitude

A relative spawn range put the drone at ~2.5 m — 0.5 m above the gate centerline, off-distribution. Holding target z = 2.0 m put every gate back in the camera frame.

03 Vision at 20 Hz

The policy was schedule-capped at 10 Hz, not compute-bound. Running it every 10th tick (20 Hz) — free on the current SoC — halved the between-gate steering lag.

04 Body-frame velocity feedback

The EKF reports world-frame velocity but the model is body-linearized; feeding one into the other made the drone crab. Derotating the feedback into the heading frame turned yaw into coordinated turns.

Concurrency model. The flown gate configuration is a single cooperative 200 Hz control loop — sense → estimate → vision → control → actuate run sequentially each tick, with the vision policy invoked inline every 10th tick and its command zero-order-held in between. The guest also ships a threaded producer/consumer variant (a high-priority control thread plus a preemptible low-priority vision thread, the DroNet pattern) for when inference must overlap control; the fused-nav course uses the inline single loop.
