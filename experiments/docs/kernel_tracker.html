Every baseline here is the model's original working curated code on the same backend — accelerated against accelerated, RVV against RVV, Gemmini against Gemmini. That is the honest way to score kernel work, and it is a much smaller number than comparing against scalar reference. For contrast, the curated-versus-scalar figures are dronet 70.5×, fused_full 33.6× and mlp_generic 3.0×; almost all of that gap is the one-time win from enabling curated kernels at all, which had been silently disabled by an unset GLOBAL_CURATED_DIR. It is a real effect but a different and far less interesting claim.

Two rows carry caveats. The Gemmini row is scored against a corrected baseline: the figures this page previously showed (8,603,884 → 7,563,328) were measured before that backend was driven to max_abs_err=0, so they compared two incorrect configurations. Both endpoints here are err = 0. And the int8-hybrid fused_full row is the best verified measurement, but several changes have landed since it was taken and it has not been re-measured on the current tree — treat it as a floor rather than a current reading.

Measurement confidence. Run-to-run variance is exactly zero — byte-identical ELFs reproduce bit-identically, confirmed on a 204-dispatch model across every per-dispatch count. Whole-model totals across rebuilds vary about 3%, and per-operator counts up to 9–10% even for operators left untouched, so any delta under roughly 10% needs the untouched-operator control before it counts. mlp_generic is deliberately listed at 1.01×: its operators sit at a shape floor where K=16–32 never fills DLEN 128, and no kernel work was attempted.

Banked: a 3.10× silu win, and a correctness scare that resolved cleanly. Replacing the memory-indexed vluxei8 gather with a register-internal vrgather.vv permute — the whole 256-entry int8 LUT fits exactly one e8m8 group at VLEN 256, so no memory traffic remains — took silu_s8 from 14,894,337 to 4,813,488 cycles (17.16 → 5.54 cyc/elem, SoL 0.46% → 1.41%). It initially reported max_abs_err=12 and was held back rather than claimed; the deciding run returned err=0, showing the error was the pre-existing layout-dependent OOB the base configuration already carried at err=11, not a fault in the new kernel. The shippable configuration is both the fastest and the clean one. The combination was predicted at 294,722,005 before measurement and came in 0.016% off.

How it got here, and one open correctness defect. The as-found ELF measured 420,385,790 with only 4 of 9 ops curated; reaching 9/9 gave 325,471,025, and switching conv to rvv_oc_blocked gave the current 304,808,537 — that variant wins 59 of 63 conv instances with all four losses under 0.9%, and it had never been evaluated on any model because vsmul_vnclip sorts ahead of it and wins first-accepted. The 21% that was silently sitting at reference was compile poisoning: one non-compiling kernel fails the whole harness build, and every op sorting alphabetically before it is quietly downgraded. Still open: the 9/9 vsmul_vnclip configuration reproduces max_abs_err=11 exactly, and it vanishes if either conv or silu is swapped — a layout-dependent out-of-bounds access that spike does not catch. It is excluded from the shipped configuration but not yet root-caused.

The backend is no longer Gemmini-bound, and the cause is a build default rather than the hardware. batchnorm (48%) and maxpool (17%) now make up 65% of what remains, with convolution down to 29%. GEMMINI.kernel_cflags is -march=rv64imafdc — no v — and GEMMINI_Q31 inherits it, so every operator without a Gemmini kernel falls back to scalar rather than to RVV. That is why batchnorm costs 82.7 cycles/element here while the existing RVV kernel does the same work in 67,555 cycles total. The bitstream carries Saturn and Gemmini together and the RVV runs use V on it happily, so this is a stale software default; enabling v and reusing the proven RVV kernels as the fallback projects to roughly 3.9M cycles, or 4.6× over RVV. That work is in progress.

The instruction-count story was wrong, and hardware said so. A static disassembly showing three working RoCC instructions against thousands of scalar glue instructions suggested the per-tile ceremony — CONFIG/FLUSH/LOOP_WS_CONFIG reissue — and the scalar index arithmetic were where Gemmini's cycles went. Two hardware experiments refuted it. Batching CPU tiles to 320 rows per call, which removes roughly 95% of that reissue for the largest layer, improved that layer by 0.31% and made smaller layers up to 7.91% worse (zero-padded mesh rows outweighing the saved ceremony), for a net 0.33% regression. Replacing per-row div/mod index decomposition with incremental tracking regressed 2.0% on spike. Both point the same way: the cost is in the im2col gather/copy and the GEMM/DMA transfer — genuine vectorisable work, not ceremony. That is an argument for the fused target rather than for anything further in the pure kernel, and it also retires the idea of separately chasing the ten FLUSHes.

What did pay off: deleting a whole tensor pass. The kernel requantized into an NHWC scratch buffer and then ran a separate full-tensor transpose to NCHW — a pass with no reuse to amortise, unlike the input transpose, which is legitimately re-read KH×KW times per pixel during im2col and was deliberately left alone. Folding the NCHW address computation into the requantize loop and writing straight into the caller's output took dronet from 20,933,650 to 19,213,428 cycles (−8.22%) and convolution itself −14.73%, with all ten conv layers moving the same direction, controls within −0.05% to +0.28%, max_abs_err=0 preserved, and about 512 KB less static RAM per call.

Fixed: add_s8 on Gemmini was wrong on yolov8n, and the validation gap was the same one as before. Isolation-tested alone it measures max_abs_err=43. It had been validated only against dronet — where every add fails the kernel's [0.5,2.0] hardware gate and falls back to scalar, so the real hardware path was never exercised at all. yolov8n's adds land inside the gate and reach tiled_resadd_auto for the first time, which uses ACC_SCALE_IDENTITY — the construction already proven non-exact. A kernel passing on a model that never runs its hot path is precisely the failure mode that let maxpool's stride-1 case and convolution's index wrap survive. It is now fixed. No exact Gemmini construction exists — every resadd entry point hardcodes full_C=false, so the raw-accumulator trick that made concatenation exact is unavailable, and a degenerate-matmul reformulation fails too because the accumulator's scale hook is a hardwired no-op and the routed operand is int8-quantized on the way in. That was proved before settling: the hardware branch and its scale gate were removed outright, and the kernel now always takes its exact int64 path. err 43 → 0, isolation-tested on yolov8n and confirmed on hardware across all six real shapes.

A naming collision made two different builds look like one, and I read a first measurement as a regression. fused_full exists in two structurally different forms that shared a backend name: an int8 hybrid (int8 conv2d_s8 encoder with an fp16 tail) and a pure fp16 build that lowers the same layers to conv2d_f16 and never touches int8. Every banked figure on this page — 165,431,489 through 4,956,577 — is the hybrid, provable from the op lists: each names conv2d_s8 and cast_i8_to_f16, ops that cannot exist in a pure fp16 graph, and a checked-in log sums bit-for-bit to one of them. The pure-fp16 build's first-ever hardware run measured 139,971,584, which I reported as a 28× regression against the hybrid's number. It was not a regression: there had never been a working pure-fp16 baseline to regress from, and the comparison was apples-to-oranges from the moment I made it. After a weight-layout fix that build now stands at 60,371,259 cycles — its first valid baseline. The remaining gap to the hybrid is the structural cost of all-fp16 convolution against int8-accelerated convolution, not a missing optimisation. The banked chain stands unretracted; the rows here now say which build they describe.

And the pure-fp16 build just got 3.37× faster, by unblocking an algorithm nobody had ever been able to run. conv2d_f16's oc_blocked candidate had sat unevaluated behind two obstacles. The anti-cheat check demanded literal oc_outer/TILE_OC identifiers because a genuine oc-blocked algorithm should carry an L1D cache-blocking outer loop, and the kernel had only a vector-width inner one — so the check was satisfied by implementing the missing loop, using the same L1D-budget formula as its proven int8 sibling, rather than by weakening the check. Underneath that sat a real numeric bug: output channels 0–4 came out bit-exact and 5–15 came out exactly zero, with the accumulator correct in all sixteen lanes but the narrowing store dropping tail lanes, and the failure moving under unrelated edits — the signature of marginal codegen. The cause was three separate re-derivations of an identically-valued vsetvl per inner iteration, one of them discarded outright with a compiler warning, left over from pre-auto-vsetvli practice. Removing them fixed it. Result on hardware: 60,371,259 → 17,931,497 cycles, convolution itself 3.69× and down from 92.6% to 84.5% of runtime, accuracy unchanged, and the algorithm now selected naturally rather than forced.

The fp16 numbers on this page had never been produced by the tool at all. Every fp16 OP_FORMULAS registration — including linear_f16 and lstm_f16, which predate this campaign — sat after if __name__ == "__main__": main(). Under the documented python -m invocation main() runs and returns before the interpreter ever reaches those lines, so fp16 operators were silently never scored by any CLI run, ever. That is why the fp16 row read 1.0% at 13% cycle coverage: not one missing formula, but a whole class of them registered past the point of execution. With the formulas moved above the guard and conv2d_f16 added, coverage reached 99.99% and blended fp16 utilisation 8.8% from the tooling fix alone, before any kernel changed. linear_f16 and lstm_f16 turned out to be scored correctly against the bandwidth roof already, at 4.7–12.7% — the honest framing for operators at arithmetic intensity ≈1.

And the operator is now 3.08× faster with bit-identical output. Pixel register tiling — the technique that won on int8 convolution — amortises the per-tap strided vlse16 weight load across up to four output pixels for 2.83×, and replacing vfwcvt+vfmacc with a single vfwmacc.vf widening MAC removed one vector operation per tap for a further 1.087×. conv2d_f16 went 15,154,377 → 4,929,652 cycles, the whole pure-fp16 model 17,931,497 → 7,710,288. Error was bit-identical across all three hardware runs, so both changes are numerically inert rather than merely within tolerance. The operator now sits at 25.2% of its widened ceiling, up from 8.2%. Doubling that ceiling by accumulating in fp16 was evaluated and rejected with a reason: the kernel spec requires an fp32 accumulator to avoid cancellation across reductions of up to 576 terms. What remains is the strided weight load itself at roughly one element per cycle — both wins cut the overhead around it, not its serialised rate.

Where that leaves the fp16 convolution. It sits at about 8.2% of its roof — and the roof is the widened 8 ops/cycle rather than the narrow 16, because the kernel converts fp16→fp32 with vfwcvt and then issues a same-width vfmacc, instead of a true narrow-accumulate vfwmacc. So the 3.7× closed the broken-vectorisation gap, not the overhead gap. What remains is per-tap fixed cost: a strided vlse16 weight load plus a vsetvli on every (ic,kh,kw) tap, contending for the core's single 16 B/cycle load port.

It had already caught the next person — twice, in opposite directions. The fused Gemmini+Saturn target's stubborn err=26 turned out to be the same mechanism inverted. gemmini_q31_rvv is a backend name split off the same day this campaign began, and CONV2D_S8's Gemmini algorithms still list only ("gemmini", "gemmini_q31") — the new string was never added. So the layout lookup returns nothing and weights ship as raw OIHW, while -DMODELBLASTER_GEMMINI_HWIO_WEIGHTS=1 is inherited unconditionally and makes every kernel on that target — the curated one and the shared reference implementation's own branch — read that blob as HWIO-packed. The proof is visible in the artifacts: gemmini_q31's weights.c carries “OIHW → HWIO (gemmini packed)” annotations and the fused target's carries none.

This retires three failed investigations at once. It explains why the error was invariant to algorithm choice (packing happens before kernel selection), why it was identical on spike and hardware (baked-in data, not timing), why it survived an all-reference build, and above all why its value drifted — 1, 3, 11, 23, 26, 40. Reading an OIHW blob as HWIO is deterministic garbage whose magnitude depends on which kernel implementation happens to walk the misaligned bytes, so unrelated edits elsewhere in the tree kept shifting the number without ever being the defect. Every hypothesis that treated the drift as evidence about kernels — including two of mine — was chasing an artifact. Adding the missing backend string gives max_abs_err=0 and an exact golden match under both an all-reference build and the full curated set. Two lines, verified, and deliberately not landed yet: another agent is editing that file right now, and landing concurrently is precisely the hazard that produced today's worst confusion.

The trip-wire is in, and it has been made to fire. A layout-agreement check now runs before weights are packed and refuses the build when conv-family operators disagree, naming both operators and both layouts instead of silently transposing. It was validated by re-introducing the known-bad edit and confirming a hard failure, then reverting — a guard that has never been tripped is not known to work. Sweeping every backend name in the tree produced no false positives.

The underlying trap is worth naming, because it will catch the next person. _conv_weight_layout_for_backend() derives one weight layout for an entire backend by consulting only conv2d_s8's algorithm list, and that layout is then applied to every 4D weight tensor on the backend. Adding an algorithm to a shared target_affinity list can therefore silently transpose an unrelated operator's weights — and because the verify step rebuilds the whole model against one corrupted weight file, the damage appears as a uniform numeric error across kernels nobody touched. Two scoped fixes are written up: a trip-wire that refuses to pack when conv-family specs disagree on layout, or threading the op name through so the layout is derived per-operator rather than per-backend.

A measurement-validity problem sits underneath several numbers on this page. Chasing an unrelated regression turned up that the pure-reference baselines are themselves divergent, and differently so per target: the plain rvv target's all-reference build measures err=92 against the golden — the same 92 logged on day one against the reference convolution — the fused gemmini_q31_rvv target measures err=26, and only the scalar target is clean at err=0. Since kernels are scored as “error versus reference”, on those targets we have been measuring against a reference that itself diverges. That does not invalidate the comparisons — a curated kernel matching its reference is still a meaningful statement, and the campaign's err=0 results were verified against the golden — but it does mean a non-zero error on those targets cannot be attributed to the kernel under test without first establishing where the reference stands. The fused target additionally carries an independent live defect: err=26 reproduces on spike and hardware even with every operator at reference, unmoved by any convolution choice. It was split off the same day and its own comment notes it was never separately regression-tested.

Landed: concatenation on Gemmini, exact and hardware-confirmed. Gemmini's mvin-time MVIN_SCALE is a real fp32 multiply with round-to-nearest-even and int8 clamp — which is exactly the per-tensor requantize the reference performs. Driving it through a degenerate tiled_matmul_auto(weight=1, full_C=true) drains the raw accumulator and bypasses the non-exact mvout scale unit entirely. cat2/3/4 came in at err=0 on both spike and FPGA, roughly 7.6× each. The constraint that makes this a narrow trick rather than a general one: only tiled_matmul_auto exposes full_C — resadd, conv and depthwise-conv do not, which is exactly why the same manoeuvre is unavailable to them. It is labelled numeric_drift rather than bit_exact on a measured ~2.3e-6 tie-breaking mismatch rate, despite measuring 0 on this model.

The correction held, and it turns a capability question into a product decision. Reading scale_and_sat confirms ReLU is a post-round clamp (exact, adds no rounding), and tiled_conv's pooling quantizes each position then maxes — identical to the reference's round-then-pool, since rounding is monotonic and commutes with max. So the earlier err=17 really was an unnecessary standalone pass. But fusing them onto a real convolution means using tiled_conv rather than im2col_full_C, and tiled_conv still carries the two-stage-versus-one-stage Q0.31 mismatch: 1.86M cycles at err=6, against 11.6M exact. The genuine choice is therefore fused, ~6× faster, with drift versus exact and slow — not whether the accelerator can do it.

And batchnorm is structurally different from the others. Its bias must be added after the multiply, while Gemmini's accumulator only supports bias before the single mvout scale. Reconciling them needs bias_int = round(bias/mult) — a second independent rounding, the same double-rounding failure already fatal for convolution, relocated to the bias. That holds regardless of fusion. The fix is upstream instead: folding BN into the preceding convolution's weights in float before quantization is standard practice and removes the operator outright — 57 calls in yolov8n, 6 in dronet — at zero runtime cost, and it benefits every backend rather than just this one.

Correction: Gemmini does these operations natively — the earlier evidence points at misuse, not a hardware limit. This page previously implied the accelerator's datapath might be unable to perform activation, pooling or data movement exactly. That reading was too pessimistic. Gemmini has ReLU as a configurable field in its execute-config applied natively on the mvout path, max pooling built into tiled_conv via pool_size/pool_stride/pool_padding, and first-class residual-add operations. The err=17 measurements came from invoking those paths as standalone operations, which forces an extra round-trip through the accumulator scale unit purely to move data — and ACC_SCALE_IDENTITY not being a true identity is a property of the scale path, which says nothing about whether the activation or pooling logic is exact.

Which reframes the question worth asking. Not “can Gemmini do relu and maxpool as separate operators” but “can convolution, batchnorm, relu and pooling be issued as one tiled_conv call?” The accelerator is designed for exactly that: activation and pooling are meant to happen on the output as it drains, not as separate tensor passes. If it holds, it removes those operators from the scalar fallback entirely, eliminates the intermediate round-trips between them, and avoids the extra pass through the scale unit that is the suspected source of the drift — attacking the correctness problem and the performance problem with one change. dronet uses ReLU, so this applies directly to the model where scalar operators now cost more than convolution itself. (yolov8n uses SiLU, which is why an existing ReLU-keyed fusion pass fires zero times there — and why fusion has never been exercised on this project.)

And the largest remaining lever is not convolution at all. The purely scalar reference operators — maxpool, six batchnorms, relu, sigmoid — sum to ~8.9M of the 19.2M remaining cycles. That is more than the entire convolution budget, and it is a missing-kernel problem rather than a glue problem.

Reversal: with both paths correct, RVV beats Gemmini by 2.8×. Driving gemmini_q31 to max_abs_err=0 — confirmed on spike and hardware — lands dronet at 20,933,650 cycles against RVV's 7,548,146. The earlier “Gemmini is 2.36× faster” compared an incorrect Gemmini (err=5/6) against an unoptimised RVV; neither side of that comparison survives. Two defects produced the error. gemmini_tiled_conv_pool and gemmini_resadd_relu both declare bit_exact and are not — isolation-tested alone, each measures err=17, because Gemmini's ACC_SCALE_IDENTITY passthrough still traverses the same fixed-point round-and-saturate unit convolution uses, so even lossless data movement picks up ~1 LSB per element. And convolution's Q0.31 drift is structural: the golden performs two-stage rounding while the mvout unit has only one round-shift-by-31, so tiled_conv pre-folds both scales into one — not fixable within that datapath. Correctness required routing around the fast-but-wrong paths, which is where the 2.8× went. That is the honest cost of correctness on this accelerator, and it is a more useful number than the one it replaces.

Correction: this row is a fused Gemmini+Saturn target, not Gemmini. Enabling the vector march on GEMMINI_Q31 and copying RVV kernels in for the operators Gemmini does not cover produced a hybrid that was still named gemmini_q31. Every “Gemmini vs RVV” figure built on it — the 5.22×, the 3,411,130 cycles, the 155× — is really Gemmini+Saturn vs Saturn. Those are valid results for a fused target and say nothing about Gemmini on its own. A gemmini-only kernel set should contain pure Gemmini code; vector instructions belong there only if the target is explicitly fused. The targets are being split: gemmini_q31 reverts to pure Gemmini plus scalar fallback, and the wins move to a separate fused target. The pure target stays measurable on purpose — its ~254M-cycle non-conv cost is a real finding about Gemmini's coverage gap, not something to paper over.

What the generated code actually does. Disassembling the pure-Gemmini dronet ELF: the whole binary contains 42 Gemmini RoCC instructions, of which only three do work — one LOOP_CONV_WS and two LOOP_WS — alongside 18 loop-config, 13 CONFIG and 10 FLUSH. There are zero MVIN/MVOUT/PRELOAD/COMPUTE ops: we drive the accelerator at its coarsest granularity, handing it an entire convolution as a single hardware-loop instruction and letting its internal state machine own all tiling, scratchpad and accumulator management. The cost of that abstraction is that dataflow and tiling are not ours to choose — which is why im2col_full_C and tiled_conv differ by 4.35×, and why gemmini_tiled_conv_pool's non-bit-exactness cannot be fixed from kernel-level code. Meanwhile kernel_conv2d_s8 is ~608 instructions of which 4 are RoCC and 0 are vector; the remainder is NCHW↔NHWC transposition, 32×32 packing and a requantize pass on the scalar core. Three accelerator instructions, thousands of scalar glue instructions — that asymmetry, not mesh utilisation, is where the Gemmini path's time goes.

Resolved: enabling the vector march turned the scalar fallback off, and it was worth 2.2× on its own. Changing GEMMINI_Q31.kernel_cflags from -march=rv64imafdc to -march=rv64imafdcv, adding the V Kconfig stanza, and reusing the existing RVV kernels for the operators Gemmini does not cover took dronet from 7,563,328 to 3,411,130 cycles. The per-operator effect is exactly what the projection predicted: batchnorm 3,654,874 → 68,005 (53.7×, within 0.7% of the standalone RVV figure), ReLU 10,414 → 543 (19.2×), maxpool 1,291,829 → 753,984 and linear 20,068 → 4,755 (both now matching RVV to within 1.2%). Convolution and add were unchanged-source controls and stayed put. Read the multiplier carefully: 5.22× is against the 17,813,964 RVV baseline current when the work started; against the RVV path's own improved 8,074,357 it is 2.37×. Correctness moved 5 → 6, and bisection has now attributed that precisely: none of it comes from the new RVV kernels. Reverting batchnorm alone left the error at 6, refuting the rounding-tie hypothesis; reverting maxpool alone to Gemmini's own kernel made it worse, at 10. That is decisive — gemmini_tiled_conv_pool carries independent numeric drift of its own (it declares BIT_EXACT and is not), and where the whole-model error lands is a matter of how that drift and conv2d_s8's documented ~1-LSB-per-layer Q0.31 fold happen to partially cancel against whichever downstream implementations are in the mix. Maxpool and ReLU are exact reduction and threshold operations and structurally cannot introduce drift; the RVV linear kernel reimplements the identical Q0.31 formula. So the error is a reshuffling of two pre-existing, already-tolerated sources well inside atol_override=128 — not a regression. Reaching 0 means fixing the Q0.31 fold and gemmini_tiled_conv_pool themselves, which is what still separates this fastest path from a shippable one.

Quantified: the scalar fallback costs more than the Gemmini convolution saves. Measuring the scalar-versus-vector ratio of the non-convolution operators directly on hardware gives batchnorm 39.9×, cat2 46.1×, cat3 39.6×, cat4 41.2× and add 2.8× — a subtotal of 111,115,288 against 4,529,957 cycles, or 24.5×. Adding an estimated ~143M for scalar silu's 868,000 expf calls, gemmini_q31's non-convolution work projects to roughly 254M cycles versus ~9.3M vectorized: about 245M wasted per inference. That is more than the entire best RVV run on yolov8n (304.8M). The conclusion is unambiguous — gemmini_q31 cannot beat RVV on yolov8n however fast its convolution is, until the elementwise operators get a vector march. The reported fix is one line in backends.py (rv64imafdc → rv64gcv) plus the RVV Kconfig overlay.

Two documented bit-exactness claims turned out to be false. gemmini_im2col_full_C is documented bit-exact but on hardware is 4.35× slower than tiled_conv and less accurate (end-to-end error 11 versus 5); spike agrees, so it is not an FPGA artifact. gemmini_tiled_conv_pool declares accuracy_class=BIT_EXACT but was host-verified against a naive reference and is not. The current Gemmini configuration carries max_abs_err=5, which is not yet reduced to zero.

Where the exact path actually spends its cycles measured, not modelled — and it refuted the plan

The numerically-exact Gemmini conv path costs 6.2× the drifting one. The working theory was that this was DMA drain traffic: full_C=true moves int32 accumulators out instead of int8, so the drain should carry 4× the bytes. That theory was used to scope the next optimization — vectorize the requantize. An rdcycle-bracketed instrumented copy of the kernel (isolation-verified numerically inert, err 0) run over all 10 dronet conv layers says the theory is wrong.

Speed-of-light roofs scripts/roofline_analysis.py · per hart

The bug class is now closed, not just the instance. An audit across every algorithm family found that convolution's third sibling, pc_direct, already carried the size_t hoisting too — so vsmul_vnclip was the only member of three missing a fix the other two had. That sharpens the diagnosis: this was never an unknown technique, it was a known fix applied to two files out of three. Four further kernels in the linear_s8 family still indexed with pure int arithmetic; exposure there was genuinely low (maximum offset N*K = 2,048 against convolution's millions, and both models using them measure err=0 on hardware) but it is the same class, so all four were hoisted as insurance. The audit now reports zero family gaps and zero int-only-indexing files. One parser detail was load-bearing: filenames must be split on registered op names longest-first, since a naive right-split turns conv2d_s8_pc_direct into op=conv2d_s8_pc and would have reported “no gaps” while hiding the very family that caused this.

Root cause found: 32-bit index arithmetic wrap — and the fix was already in a sibling file. rvv_conv2d_s8_vsmul_vnclip indexed with all-int arithmetic while oc_blocked used (size_t) casts. BSS buffers live above 0x80000000, so their low 32 bits are negative as an int32 and the index wraps. That one fact explains every observation that had resisted explanation: why the symptom migrated between err=1, err=3 and a Store/AMO fault as the binary shifted; why spike never saw it; why dronet and fused_full passed while yolov8n failed (smaller footprints, buffers below the boundary); and why static review found no bounds violation — the indexing is correct, the arithmetic overflows before it indexes. Two earlier theories are retired by this: the vta/vma tail-lane divergence, and the reading of mtval=6400 as a null base pointer (that value matching a buffer size was coincidence). The fix was documented in oc_blocked's own comment and its sibling never received it, which makes this a curation failure rather than a kernel-authoring one. Porting it lifted yolov8n's convolution from 291,764,785 to 164,725,311 cycles (1.771×) at err=0, took convolution from 23.4% to 43.9% of the achievable ceiling. The vsse8 store win, deliberately handed back to change one variable at a time, was then restored: the corrected kernel now matches the fast-but-broken one to 0.17% on dronet, confirming the store form was never implicated. And it settles the one-kernel-per-op question: per-shape dispatch was never needed.

A shared kernel had verification gaps that no single model could reveal. maxpool2d_s8 was rewritten to replace a vlse8 stride-2 gather with a contiguous vle16 plus vnsrl split — one 16-bit load serves two taps, so a K=3 window costs two contiguous loads instead of three gathers, taking the load from 3.56 to 1.04 cycles/element and the op from 748,163 to 217,609 cycles (3.44×). But yolov8n's three maxpools are SW=1/K=5/P=2 while every existing verify shape was SW=2/PW=0 — so the stride-1 and padded-boundary paths of a shared kernel were exercised by no model's build, and the rewrite would have shipped into yolov8n on dronet-only evidence. The SPPF shape has been added and the op now verifies at five shapes with the ELF md5 unchanged, proving codegen was unaffected. This is the one-kernel-per-op hazard crossing agent boundaries, which is worse than the within-model form because no single agent's testing covers it. An audit of extra_shapes across the other shared operators is now the priority ahead of further kernel work.

The fp16 roof is wrong too, and in the other direction. Tracing the RTL (SatGemDualSmallTacitConfig → WithRocketVectorUnit(vLen=256, dLen=128)), nTandemFMA = dLenB/8 = 2 segments per cycle each holding 4 fp16 lanes, giving a narrow pure-fp16-accumulate peak of 8 MAC/cycle (16 flop/cycle) — not the 32 ops/cycle previously assumed. Widening through vfwmacc to an fp32 accumulator halves that to 4 MAC/cycle. So the fp16 utilisation figures reported as 5–11% are really 10–22% of the correct roof. Bandwidth is the tighter constraint: there is exactly one LoadSequencer for the whole core at vMemDataBits = 128b = 16 B/cycle, and the original kernels issued two independent vle16 streams per K-chunk contending for that single port, capping them near 4 MAC/cycle. With fmaPipeDepth = 4 and one loop-carried accumulator per output, the pipeline could not be hidden either. Measured utilisation of 4.8–11.3% sits well below both roofs, so this family is not roofed out — the headroom is real. Note the roofline script itself applies a single dtype-blind 64 ops/cycle constant to every operator, which is wrong by 4–8× for int8 and fp16 alike; every SoL figure on this page inherits that error until it is fixed.

Acting on it: blocking linear_f16 by 4 in N and lstm_f16 by 4 in the gate loop — hoisting the redundant input reload out of the inner loop, with B=4 chosen to match both the LSTM's natural gate count and fmaPipeDepth — took fused_full from 5,090,722 to 4,922,762 cycles at unchanged error. Per-instance: vision_fc −12.4% and lstm_l0 −7.2 to −7.8% with clean controls (≤0.94%); depth_fc −11.7 to −12.7% but with its own control swinging +11%, so moderate confidence only; lstm_l1/l2 showed no established effect and are not claimed.

Correction: the roof these percentages were measured against was wrong, by 8×. The SoL column below scored int8 MAC work against 64 ops/cycle. That figure is unreachable on this build. The backend compiles -march=rv64gcv with no Zvqdotq extension, so vwmacc is the only int8 MAC path available and it writes 32 bits per MAC — which at DLEN 128 caps throughput at 4 MAC/cycle, or 12.5% of the nominal peak. Every convolution SoL number here was therefore pessimistic by a factor of eight. Rescored against the achievable ceiling, convolution runs at 53.9% on dronet and 51.3% on fused_full — not the 3–6% the raw column suggests. The practical consequence is a changed conclusion about where to spend effort: remaining RVV headroom on convolution is at most about 2×, not 16×, and going beyond that requires Gemmini or a dot-product extension rather than more kernel tuning. The percentages in the tables below are still against the nominal roof; multiply by eight for the achievable one.

Measured constants, not just roofs. Two costs have been pinned on this hardware and both are now triage rules. A vluxei8 memory-indexed gather runs at 11.9 cycles/element against 1.7 for an equivalent f32 arithmetic chain — about 7×, established by a registered prediction (a LUT variant was forecast at 7.5× slower and measured 5.64×). That single constant explains why silu sits at 17.2 cyc/elem while batchnorm sits at 2.1 on identical tensors, so “is this a LUT/gather kernel?” belongs alongside runtime share and SoL when picking targets. Separately, a vnclip costs roughly 50 cycles per channel, because GCC emits csrwi vxrm,0 and hoists it only to the channel loop; a single saturating vfncvt.x.f.w gets the same saturation with zero CSR writes, which was worth 55.7× on dronet's batchnorm.

Utilisation is clock-independent — frequency cancels between measured GOPS and the roof — so the 20 MHz bitstream reports the same % of peak a 60 MHz one would. Absolute GOPS figures do still need the real clock, which the script currently hardcodes to 1000 MHz.
